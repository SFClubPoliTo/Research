The historical GLD return series supplies a different diagnostic from the
option cross-section. It is observed under the historical data-generating
process and is used only to test Gaussian adequacy, not to estimate a physical
drift or to rank option-pricing models. With
$r_t=100[\log S_t-\log S_{t-1}]$, the null hypothesis is that $r_t$ is
normally distributed. Figure~\ref{fig:t8-return-normality} shows both the
central concentration and the tail departures; Shapiro--Wilk~\cite{ShapiroWilk1965},
Jarque--Bera and D'Agostino $K^2$ tests (Table~\ref{tab:t8-return-normality})
all reject. In a large, heterogeneous financial sample rejection is
unsurprising: it motivates a variance-and-jump model stack but does not
identify a preferred option model.

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white,colframe=sfblue,boxrule=0.5pt,arc=1mm,left=3mm,right=3mm,title={Calibration fragility}]
\small
The Feller condition is imposed as a hard constraint. It binds at the optimum
for Bates and Bates--Hawkes; Heston sits at the boundary within numerical
tolerance (gap 0.0013 against $2\kappa\theta_v\approx0.426$). Without the
constraint the Heston optimum moves into the Feller-violating region
($\xi\approx1.16$) with a \FellerRelaxedLossRatio\% lower loss. The Heston loss changes by less than 3\% for
$\kappa$ between \KappaFlatLow{} and \KappaFlatHigh, so mean reversion is
weakly identified. With $q=0.40\%$ the Bates jump intensity is \BatesLambda{}
a year rather than zero, but jump and Hawkes parameters rest on 64 calls and
are not individually identified. The carry itself is supported by independent
evidence: COMEX gold futures against the synchronous spot imply a GLD yield of
\FuturesYieldLow--\FuturesYieldHigh\% at 0.5--0.8 years. With the fee as carry
the fit deteriorates: in a separate carry-sensitivity refit (local SLSQP
from one common start, so its levels differ from
Table~\ref{tab:t8-model-errors}) the Heston price RMSE rises from
USD~\CarryPriceRMSEZero{} at $q=0$ to \CarryPriceRMSEFee{} at $q=0.40\%$, and the loss increases in every moneyness bucket. Call-only
surfaces cannot separate carry from skew, so the futures-implied fee is used
rather than a fitted carry.
\end{tcolorbox}
