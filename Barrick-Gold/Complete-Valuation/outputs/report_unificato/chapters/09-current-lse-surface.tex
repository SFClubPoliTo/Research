\section{Current surface and predictive audit}
\label{sec:current-surface-audit}

The valuation uses the 2 September 2026 GLD call snapshot, distinct from the
historical source-study examples. It applies DTE $\geq75$ (expiries 79--653
days); the CC64 geometry and counts are given with
Figure~\ref{fig:t8-market-inputs}. Figure~\ref{fig:gld-current-option-surface}
shows the surface and the 64 selected nodes.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering

\caption{GLD implied-volatility surface on 2 September 2026 with the 64 selected

The same-date U.S. Treasury curve fits 14 tenor observations with an NSS
RMSE of 2.0608 basis points against the continuously compounded par-yield proxy.
This is not a bootstrapped zero-coupon term structure. Option-maturity rates
and quarterly integral-preserving forward rates derive from this common curve.

With the GLD sponsor fee treated as carry ($q=0.40\%$), the current-snapshot
IV RMSE is \CalBSIVRMSE{} basis points for Black--Scholes, \CalHIVRMSE{} for
Heston, \CalBIVRMSE{} for Bates--Poisson and \CalWIVRMSE{} for
Bates--Hawkes; the ordering follows from the nested fits
(Table~\ref{tab:t8-model-errors}) and is not evidence of predictive or
economic superiority. The 7 September fits with
$q=0$ (\CalBSIVRMSELegacy, \CalHIVRMSELegacy, \CalBIVRMSELegacy{} and
\CalWIVRMSELegacy~bp) target different implied volatilities and are not a
like-for-like comparison. The Hawkes branching ratio is \HawkesBranching,
below the stationarity boundary, with a kernel half-life $\log 2/\beta$ of
\HawkesHalfLifeDays{} days and a stationary mean intensity of
\HawkesMeanIntensity{} jumps a year; these are risk-neutral fitted parameters
on 64 calls, not measured event persistence.

The dense-only rolling panel contains 31 accepted dates and \OosDates{}
forecast origins. Each origin is recalibrated on its own CC64 sample with
$q=0.40\%$, nested starts and the same optimizer search for every model, and
is evaluated on the next available dense date, yielding \OosObservations{}
common forecasts. Variance and Hawkes intensity are projected to the target
date through their mean-reversion laws, while the target date's GLD spot and
Treasury curve condition the repricing; the test therefore measures conditional
risk-neutral surface stability, not an origin-only forecast of spot, rates and
options. Date-equal RMSE is the mean of the daily RMSEs. Date-equal IV RMSE is \OosBSRMSE, \OosHRMSE, \OosBRMSE{} and
\OosWRMSE{} basis points for the same four models, against \OosPersistenceRMSE{}
for interpolated observed-IV persistence on its own support. Every
$R^2_{OOS}$ against persistence in Table~\ref{tab:current-dense-oos} is
negative: no model beats persistence. Bates--Hawkes comes closest; its
daily loss difference from persistence is not significant (Diebold--Mariano
test~\cite{DieboldMariano1995} with Newey--West errors, $p=\OosWDMPersistenceP$), and neither is its advantage over Bates--Poisson
($p=\OosDMHawkesVsBP$), whereas its advantages over Heston and
Black--Scholes are significant ($p$-values of \OosDMHawkesVsHP{} and
\OosDMHawkesVsBSP{} to three decimals). Bates--Hawkes is the primary
structural illustration because it is the richest law, not because of this
ranking. None of these benchmark comparisons establishes physical gold
forecasting accuracy. The historical August fixed-target exercise
(Table~\ref{tab:online-oos}) is not interchangeable with this dense-date
design.
