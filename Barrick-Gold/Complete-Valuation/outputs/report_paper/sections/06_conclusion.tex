\section*{Conclusion}

Holding operations and corporate assumptions fixed isolates the effect of gold-price model choice. On a valuation clock that counts only flows after 2 September 2026, the four option-implied laws give median proxies of USD~\ValMedianLow--\ValMedianHigh~billion, inside the Monte Carlo range of a single model, while the perpetuity after 2030 carries about \TerminalShare\% of the mean. The model choice therefore matters less for this proxy than numerical precision and, above all, the terminal assumptions.

In rolling repricing Bates--Hawkes has the lowest error but is statistically indistinguishable from Bates and from IV-surface persistence. Treating the GLD fee as carry is economically required and corroborated by gold futures, yet it worsens the in-sample fit, which signals that call-only surfaces cannot identify the forward. The copper extension adds about USD~\CuDeltaLow--\CuDeltaHigh~billion of explicit flows; its sparse rolling experiment does not beat persistence either.

The contribution is an auditable interdisciplinary framework, not a certified value. The next required results are a validated physical commodity forecast linked to Barrick's realised prices, a forward identified from puts or box spreads, stochastic operating inputs, a reconciled FCFF and equity bridge, and a finite-resource terminal closure.
